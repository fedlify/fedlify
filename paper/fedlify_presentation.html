1 · What is the project about

Training health AI across hospitals — without moving the data

  • Setting: reliable, generalizable health AI needs data from many institutions (EHR, imaging, genomics, biosignals).
  • Barrier: privacy law, governance, and institutional silos block raw-data sharing — so most models are trained on a single site.
  • Federated learning (FL) lets sites train a shared model while data stay local — but running a real study is operationally hard.
  • Goal: Fedlify is a platform that manages the full lifecycle of a federated study — intake → governance → configuration → execution → aggregation → reporting.

The core idea

Treat federated learning as a governed workflow, not just a training loop — with an AI agent to reduce coordination burden and humans in control of every decision.

Built on

Next.js application layer · NVFlare federated runtime · Gitea + S3 · role-based governance · an LLM coordination agent.

  • The bottleneck is operational, not algorithmic — orchestration, governance, traceability, and deployment, not the training math.
  • Evidence from real deployments (Nordic–Baltic network, computational pathology) shows barriers are organizational, regulatory, and socio-technical.
  • Who benefits: health researchers, hospitals unable to share data, and patients — through more generalizable, equitable models.

3 · What is novel

Controlled agency: the agent prepares, humans decide

Governance-first design

Study, site, and platform roles (RBAC) with explicit approval gates for activation and pipeline execution. Governance is a first-class layer, not an afterthought.

Agent as versioned "skill cards"

Each skill = trigger, inputs, instruction, allowed tools, output schema, validation tests, stop conditions, human gate, and evidence basis — not an open-ended chat prompt.

  • plan → act → check → revise loop that turns study goals into inspectable platform artifacts.
  • The agent can: gather metadata, draft plans, generate config from approved templates, summarize logs, draft reports.
  • The agent cannot: approve collaborations, touch patient data, execute studies, release kits, or override governance.
  • Every mutation is audited; artifacts are versioned, signed/checksummed, and reproducible.

4 · How the model addresses the problem & results so far

Closing the operational & governance gap left by FL runtimes

Framework comparison across 9 dimensions — governance & orchestration are where existing tools fall short:

DimensionExisting FL frameworksFedlify focus
Federated trainingstrongreuse (NVFlare)
Deployment readinessvariescontainerized
Workflow orchestrationpartialcore
Reproducibility / tracepartialcore
Governance supportweakestcore
  • Framework study: 13 frameworks scored via a 5-submetric rubric, with a blinded, evidence-locked LLM cross-check (GPT-5.5 + Claude Sonnet) and human adjudication of disagreements.
  • Simulated multi-site study ran end-to-end across all sites — study setup, onboarding, orchestration, execution, aggregation — with reduced coordination overhead.
  • Reproducibility: repeated runs of the same workflow gave consistent results, backed by structured logging & config versioning.

5 · Next steps

From simulated validation to real clinical deployment

Build out the agent

Implement the seven skill cards end-to-end and validate guardrails, stop conditions, and human-approval gates in the live platform.

Real-world deployment

Move beyond simulation to live multi-hospital pilots; integrate with clinical data systems and institutional infrastructure.

  • Strengthen system-level evaluation — coordination overhead, reliability, and reproducibility metrics at larger scale.
  • Expand privacy/security options (secure aggregation, DP) as validated template choices.
  • Harden the audit & reproducibility package for real governance and ethics review.
  • Broaden framework/template support beyond NVFlare where useful.
